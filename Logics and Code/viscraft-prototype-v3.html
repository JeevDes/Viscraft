Processing time varies by destination and by season. For most Schengen countries the embassy takes roughly 10 to 15 working days once your appointment is done, though peak summer and December travel can stretch that further. The UK, USA and Canada work on their own timelines and can take several weeks depending on appointment availability in your city.

On our side, we usually have your documentation reviewed and packaged within 48 hours of receiving everything, and we book the earliest appointment slot available. Rush handling is available if you are working to a tight departure date — tell us the date and we will tell you honestly whether it is achievable before you pay.

Yes, absolutely. A previous rejection does not disqualify you, and it does not have to be disclosed as a permanent mark against you — but it does mean the next application needs to be noticeably stronger than the last one.

The first thing we do is read your rejection letter closely. Embassies issue a coded reason, and that code tells us precisely what went wrong: insufficient funds, unclear travel purpose, weak ties to your home country, or incomplete documentation. Most rejections come down to one or two fixable gaps rather than anything fundamental about you as an applicant.

From there we rebuild the application around that weakness — stronger financial evidence, a clearer day-by-day itinerary, better proof of your intent to return — and add a cover letter that addresses the earlier refusal directly rather than hoping the officer overlooks it.

Reach out to us as soon as you notice it. If the application has not been submitted yet, almost anything can be corrected at no cost — a spelling error in a name, a wrong passport number, an incorrect date of travel.

If it has already been submitted, the options depend on how far along it is. Before your biometrics appointment, corrections can usually still be made in person on the day. After submission to the embassy, minor clerical errors can often be raised through a written correction request, while a material error may mean withdrawing and reapplying.

Either way, do not try to fix it by submitting a second application in parallel — duplicate applications are a common cause of outright rejection. Call us first and we will tell you the cleanest route.
