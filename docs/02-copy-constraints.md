# Copy constraints — non-negotiable

These are binding, not stylistic preferences — a slip here is a legal/fraud-marker risk, not a tone miss. Applies to every page and every CMS entry (including testimonials), everywhere on the Viscraft site.

1. No outcome guarantees. Never "assured", "guaranteed", "100%", "we get you the visa" — anywhere, including testimonials.
2. No success-rate figures at all. No verified records exist yet.
3. A disclaimer does not rescue a misleading headline. The claim itself must be accurate.
4. No claimed credential, accreditation, affiliation or partnership. Embassies do not authorise agents; implying otherwise is a fraud marker.
5. No job-placement or employment-migration language. No "work visas", "jobs abroad", "settle overseas" — out of scope, wrong enquiries entirely.
6. No invented testimonials. Use clearly-labelled placeholders only.
7. No stock imagery presented as our work or as client outcomes.

**Why**: the Indian visa consultancy market has a real fraud problem, and these are the exact patterns fraudulent operators use — avoiding them isn't just legal hygiene, it's the product's core trust claim (see [`01-overview.md`](01-overview.md) — Positioning).

**How to apply**: before calling any page done, grep it for: `guarantee`, `assured`, `100%`, `success rate`, `approved`, `authorised`, `partner`. Report every hit before marking the page complete — don't silently fix and move on.
